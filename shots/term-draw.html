If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face. ## 1. The idea Software is most convincing when it is doing its job in front of you. Instead of describing features, put small, true, working fragments of the product on the page: the actual control, with real content, responding to the pointer. A colour picker's loupe reads the page's own background and prints its value. A list tool lets you tick an item right there. A type-scale plugin sets the page's own headings. Find the one interaction that is the product and make the hero that interaction; then show each further capability as another fragment at real size. No dashboard collage, no browser frame, no screenshots shrunk until unreadable. Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>." Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's. Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4). ## 2. Composition Hero: a short plain headline (4–6vw, left-aligned, upper-left third) and one sentence; the rest of the first screen is the main fragment at actual interface size, sitting directly on the page or on one flat stage colour, large enough to read every label. The fragment is built in HTML and works. Below, capabilities are a single-column sequence, each a row of two parts on a 5/7 split: a two-line statement on one side and the relevant fragment on the other, with the fragments varying in size and shape so the column never becomes a zigzag of equal blocks. Fragments are cropped pieces of interface (a menu, a row, a control, a panel edge), not whole windows. One section pins a fragment while three short statements change it. ## 3. Type — Switzer A single roman weight of a Swiss neo-grotesk at two or three modest sizes; position and grey value do the work that size and bold usually do. Suits architects, galleries, studios and any quiet page where photographs carry the scale. ```html <link rel="preconnect" href="https://api.fontshare.com"> <link rel="preconnect" href="https://cdn.fontshare.com" crossorigin> <link href="https://api.fontshare.com/v2/css?f[]=switzer@400,500&display=swap" rel="stylesheet"> ``` - Display: **Switzer** — weight 400 · tracking -0.03em · leading 1.1 · sentence case; headings stay small (28–40px) and at most one line jumps to 5vw - Text: **Switzer** — 400 for everything, 500 only for names and links · leading 1.45 · tracking -0.01em; hierarchy by ink against mid-grey ```css :root { --step-small: clamp(0.8125rem, 0.79rem + 0.1vw, 0.875rem); --step-body: clamp(1.0625rem, 1rem + 0.25vw, 1.1875rem); --step-lead: clamp(1.25rem, 1.1rem + 0.7vw, 1.625rem); --step-head: clamp(2rem, 1.3rem + 3.2vw, 4rem); } ``` The marketing type matches the product's interface type so the fragments and the page feel like one thing. Headline modest; interface text at its true size (13–15px) inside fragments. The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face. The display size itself comes from the hero blueprint below. ## 4. Colour — Bottle ink, yellow ```css :root { --bg: #f7f7f7; --surface: #ebebeb; --ink: #0d2a1c; --muted: #5f6b64; --accent: #f6e016; --accent-ink: #0d2a1c; --field-1: #f6e016; --field-1-ink: #0d2a1c; } ``` Off-white ground with all type in a near-black bottle green instead of black. Yellow is the only saturated note: the button or one small object on most screens, and one later section that turns fully yellow. Neutral ground. The product's own interface colours provide the colour. At most one flat stage colour behind fragments. ## 5. Imagery **This subject is software and there are no screenshots, so its pictures are its own interface.** Wherever this sheet says photograph, image or object, build a believable fragment of the product in HTML at real size (real labels, numbers that add up, one item hovered or selected) by the app-screen rules (references/app-screens.md: deal its style once with `node <skill>/scripts/draw.mjs --app --kind <kind> --keep palette,type --out .taste-skill/app`, and build it with assets/app.css and assets/charts.js), put the attribute `data-ui` on its root, and crop or scale it the way the blueprint treats a photograph: a full-bleed close crop, a tile, the fixed half of a split. Where a fragment makes no sense, use a flat colour field with type on it. Never a fake dashboard of grey bars, never a device frame. If the product works on something physical (ships, plants, buildings, a city), one real picture of that world can carry a section: find it with `node C:/Users/admin/.dsh/skills/design-taste-frontend/scripts/assets.mjs "<that world>"`, download it with `--get`, credit it on the page, and never let it pass for the product or its customers. The interface fragments are the main imagery: build them carefully with real labels, real numbers and sensible states (one hovered, one selected). Around them, two or three real pictures of the customer's world (found with scripts/assets.mjs), never stock of people at laptops. **Floor (the inspector checks it):** at least four real pictures on the page, usually six to twelve; SVG drawings do not count. One moving picture where an honest clip exists: `node <skill>/scripts/assets.mjs --video "<query>"`, used as a muted loop with a poster frame. Whatever this direction says about pictures, it is carried out with real ones. ## 6. Hero — Fragment on a shaped stage A neutral ground. The headline occupies the upper-left third: two lines at clamp(52px, 5.2vw, 80px), medium weight, flush left on columns 1–6 at a 40px margin, starting 150px down. One supporting sentence at 18px, max 40ch, follows, then the single action as a small rectangular button. The rest of the screen belongs to one flat stage in a saturated colour: it spans columns 5–12, begins 42% down, and bleeds off the right and bottom edges. Its top-left corner is cut with the product's own shape using `clip-path` (a notch, a chamfer or a tab two columns wide), the only decoration on the page. On the stage, 56px in from its left and top edges, sits the main fragment of the interface at true size with labels at 13–15px, built in HTML and working: one panel or control about 620px wide, cropped by the bottom edge, never a whole window and never inside a browser or laptop frame. Mark its root with data-ui. Navigation is a plain row at 28px from the top: wordmark left and four text links right at 15px. Nothing overlaps the headline. On a phone: The headline sits under the wordmark at 10vw on two lines, followed by the sentence at 16px and the button. The stage comes next at full width, bleeding off both sides and the bottom, its corner cut kept at 72px wide. The fragment sits 16px inside it at true size; if it is wider than the screen the right edge crops it rather than scaling it down.
